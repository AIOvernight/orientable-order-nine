# Bibliographic coverage and limits of the novelty assessment

Review date: 15 October 2026. Conclusion: **the two equalities for M_k(9) were not found in the results actually examined**. This does not prove absolute priority. The manuscript identifies the global equalities as the candidate new contribution; the general mechanisms used have antecedents.

The manuscript's comparative counts are our calculations from published constructions. They are reproduced by exact inclusion–exclusion. They are not attributed to the papers as global optimality theorems, and simplified lower-bound guarantees are not confused with the complete support of a construction.

## Dai, Martin, Robshaw, and Wild (1993)

Pages 97–115 of *Orientable Sequences*, in *Cryptography and Coding III*, were read directly through the [authorized volume reader](https://archive.org/details/cryptographycodi0000unse/page/97/mode/1up). The original confirms the author order Dai, Martin, Robshaw, and Wild. It studies binary sequences, establishes bounds, and presents a cycle-joining construction. Its table on p. 114 leaves the binary order-nine maximum unknown, with bounds 126 and 206. It does not contain the formulas proposed here for k≥3. Its construction techniques are credited as prior work.

## Concatenation trees

The 24-page [author manuscript](https://www.socs.uoguelph.ca/~sawada/papers/ConcatTrees.pdf) was examined, and its relevant section was compared with [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12405v3). Review included the introduction, the nonbinary framework, the hypotheses of Theorems 3 and 14, and §5.4. The general framework admits nonbinary alphabets but starts from a given cycle-joining tree; it does not determine the maximum over all orientable supports. The application in §5.4 is binary.

The [publisher DOI 10.1016/j.dam.2026.07.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2026.07.022) corresponds to Discrete Applied Mathematics 395, pp. 35–55, with issue date 31 December 2026. Identity between versions is not certified; the issue date also does not establish when the paper became available online.

## Other leads examined

Mitchell–Wild, [Negative Avoiding Sequences](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25286v2), first submitted 26 March 2026 and revised 23 April 2026, was added during the same-day refresh. Definition 1.3 and the main existence theorems concern exclusion of componentwise negatives, rather than reversals. Section 8 explicitly distinguishes negative avoiding, orientable, and negative orientable sequences; it states that orientable maximum periods remain unresolved apart from small orders. This is an older related result, not a publication from the hours covered by the refresh, and no equivalent order-nine orientable theorem was identified in the examined passages.

Mitchell–Wild, [Special orientable sequences](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/94/ajc_v94_p122.pdf), Australasian Journal of Combinatorics 94(1) (2026), 122–144, was reviewed, especially its recursive constructions. The explicit families examined do not give the two proposed equalities. This does not exclude every possible seed or modification of recursive methods.

The [sequence-construction project](https://debruijnsequence.org/db/orientable) provided context and links. Statements about known maxima in its binary section were not extrapolated to larger alphabets.

## Comparison criteria and limitations

An equivalent result must concern the same object: a circular word, distinct windows, no palindromes or reversal pairs, a maximum over all supports, and the full stated domain of k. Coverage with repetitions, linear sequences, negative orientability, and optima restricted to a family are not automatically identified with this maximum.

The equalities were not found in the results actually examined. The publisher-version comparison described above and an independent assessment of priority remain pending, including citations, unindexed material, and possible equivalences not yet identified. This limited search is not presented as proof of worldwide priority.

## Bibliographic identification correction

*New orientable sequences* (Acta Informatica, March 2026; preprint 2507.02526) and *Constructing orientable and negative orientable sequences with asymptotically optimal period* (arXiv 2603.18646v2, August 2026) are different works. The table's bound U is attributed to the former; our support count B is calculated from the latter's construction. The manuscript preserves this distinction.
